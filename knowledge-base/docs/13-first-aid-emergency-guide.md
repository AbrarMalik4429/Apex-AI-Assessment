# First aid for emergencies

Source guidance comes from international first-aid bodies (American Heart Association, Red Cross, Mayo Clinic, ANZCOR). Saudi emergency numbers are 997 (ambulance) and 911 (unified). This guide supports, and never replaces, emergency services and trained help.

## Rules for the assistant when someone needs first aid
Always tell the person to call **997 (or 911)** first, or to have someone else call while they start first aid. Give the steps in short numbered lines, one action at a time, without asking many questions. Tell them to follow the emergency operator's instructions, which take priority over this guide. Do not diagnose, do not suggest medicines or doses except where a step below states it, and after giving the steps escalate to a human team member. State that first aid is not a substitute for medical care. `[DEMO_CONFIG]`

## Adult CPR: when to start and how to do hands-only CPR
Start CPR on a teen or adult who is unresponsive and not breathing normally (no breathing or only gasping). Call 997 or 911, or send someone to call and to fetch an AED. Kneel beside the person on a firm flat surface. Place the heel of one hand on the centre of the chest and the other hand on top, elbows straight, shoulders above your hands. Push straight down at least 5 cm (2 inches) but no more than 6 cm (2.4 inches), at 100 to 120 pushes per minute, roughly the beat of "Stayin' Alive". Let the chest rise fully between pushes and keep interruptions under 10 seconds. Continue until help takes over, an AED is ready, the person shows obvious signs of life, or you are too exhausted. Untrained rescuers, or anyone unsure, should do hands-only CPR without mouth-to-mouth breaths. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Adult CPR with rescue breaths for trained rescuers
A trained rescuer who is confident can give 30 chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths, and repeat. After the compressions, open the airway by tilting the head back and lifting the chin (do not tilt if a neck injury is possible), pinch the nose, seal your mouth over theirs, and blow for about 1 second so the chest visibly rises. If the chest does not rise, retilt the head and try again. Use an AED as soon as one arrives. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Child CPR
For a child who is unresponsive and not breathing normally: shout for help and have someone call 997 or 911 and fetch an AED. If you are alone, give about 2 minutes of CPR (five cycles of 30 compressions and 2 breaths) and then call. Use the heel of one or two hands on the centre of the chest, push about 5 cm (2 inches), or one third of the chest depth, at 100 to 120 per minute, then give 2 gentle breaths after every 30 compressions. Open the airway to a slightly past-neutral head position. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Infant CPR
For a baby under about one year who is unresponsive and not breathing normally: check by flicking the sole of the foot, shout for help, and have someone call 997 or 911. If you are alone, give about 2 minutes of CPR before calling. Lay the baby on a firm surface. Place two fingers on the centre of the chest just below the nipple line and push about 4 cm (1.5 inches), or one third of the chest depth, at 100 to 120 per minute. After every 30 compressions give 2 gentle breaths, covering the baby's nose and mouth with your mouth, about 1 second each. Do not start compressions if the baby is breathing normally, coughing or moving. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Using an AED (defibrillator)
Use an automated external defibrillator as soon as one is available. Turn it on and follow its spoken prompts, apply the pads as shown, and keep doing CPR until the device is ready to analyse. If the person is wet, move them out of water and dry the chest first. Do not touch the person while the device analyses or delivers a shock. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Choking in an adult or child over one year
If the person can cough forcefully, encourage them to keep coughing. If they cannot speak, cough or breathe, get someone to call 997 or 911 and act at once: lean the person forward and give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand; then give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (stand behind, fist above the belly button, pull sharply inwards and upwards). Alternate back blows and abdominal thrusts until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive. For a pregnant or very large person, use chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts. If they become unresponsive, lower them to the floor and start CPR with compressions. Only remove an object you can actually see; never do blind finger sweeps. If you are choking alone, call for help and give yourself abdominal thrusts against a firm object such as a chair back. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Choking in a baby under one year
If the baby cannot cry, cough or breathe, call 997 or 911 (or have someone call). Hold the baby face down along your forearm or thigh with the head lower than the chest, supporting the head and jaw. Give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades. Then turn the baby face up and give up to 5 chest thrusts with two fingers on the centre of the chest, about 4 cm (1.5 inches) deep. Repeat. Do not use abdominal thrusts or blind finger sweeps on a baby. If the baby becomes unresponsive, start infant CPR. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Severe bleeding
Call 997 or 911. Press firmly and directly on the wound with a clean cloth or dressing. For life-threatening limb bleeding, or bleeding that direct pressure cannot control, first-aid guidelines say to use a tourniquet as soon as one is available, or to pack the wound where the location allows. Keep the person lying down and warm and stay with them. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Heart attack and chest pain
Warning signs include chest pain or pressure, shortness of breath, nausea, sweating, or pain in the jaw, arm or back. Symptoms can be milder in women, people with diabetes and older adults. Call 997 or 911 immediately. Do not drive yourself unless there is no other option. First-aid guidelines say an alert adult with chest pain may be encouraged to chew and swallow aspirin (162 to 325 mg) while waiting, unless they are allergic or have been told not to take it; do not give aspirin if stroke signs are present. If the person becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally, start CPR. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Stroke
Use F.A.S.T. (BE-FAST adds Balance and Eyes): Face drooping on one side, Arm weakness or numbness, Speech slurred or confused, Time to call 997 or 911 immediately. Note the time symptoms began and tell the operator, including any fall or head injury. Do not give food or drink. Do not give aspirin, which can worsen a bleeding stroke. If the person is drowsy or vomiting, place them on their side. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Seizure
Stay calm and time the seizure. Clear hard or dangerous objects away and cushion the head. Do not restrain the person and do not put anything in their mouth. When the shaking stops, roll them onto their side and check breathing. Call 997 or 911 if it lasts longer than 5 minutes, repeats, is the person's first seizure, happens in water, causes injury, the person is pregnant or diabetic, or they do not regain consciousness. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Burns
Remove the person from the source of the burn. Cool the burn with cool running water for at least 20 minutes, starting as soon as possible (it can still help within about 3 hours). Then cover loosely with a clean non-stick dressing. Call 997 or 911 for large or deep burns, or burns on the face, hands, feet or genitals. For chemical burns, flush thoroughly with water and call 937 for poisoning guidance. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Heatstroke and heat exhaustion
Heatstroke is a life-threatening emergency: confusion, fainting, seizures, a fast pulse, and hot skin (dry, or sometimes still sweating). Call 997 or 911. Move the person to a cool place out of the sun, remove extra clothing, and cool the whole body with cold water, sponging or spraying with fanning, and ice packs on the neck, armpits and groin. Give cool water only if they are fully alert and can swallow safely. Do not give aspirin or paracetamol (acetaminophen). Be ready to start CPR. Heat exhaustion (heavy sweating, cool clammy skin, dizziness, weakness) needs shade, cooling and cool drinks if alert, and medical help; treat any confusion or hot dry skin as heatstroke. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)
Signs include hives, swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, wheezing or trouble breathing, vomiting, dizziness or collapse. Call 997 or 911. If the person has an epinephrine (adrenaline) auto-injector, use it immediately into the outer thigh, through clothing if needed, holding it in place for the time the device states. Lay the person flat with legs raised (sit them up if breathing is difficult). If symptoms persist after 5 to 10 minutes, EMS has not arrived and a second auto-injector is available, give a second dose. Give the used injector to the ambulance crew. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Recovery position for an unresponsive person who is breathing
If a person is unresponsive but breathing normally, or is vomiting or drowsy after a stroke or seizure, roll them onto their side so the airway stays clear, keep checking their breathing, and call 997 or 911. If breathing stops or becomes abnormal, start CPR. `[GUIDELINE]`

## Asthma attack and low blood sugar
Asthma: help the person sit upright and use their reliever inhaler (with a spacer if available); if symptoms do not improve after 5 minutes or get worse, call 997 or 911. Low blood sugar (sweating, shaking, confusion): if they can swallow, give 15 to 20 grams of fast sugar such as glucose tablets or a sugary drink, wait 15 minutes and repeat if needed; if they are drowsy or cannot swallow, call 997 or 911 and give nothing by mouth. `[TYPICAL]`

## Suspected spinal injury, fractures and sprains
If a spinal injury is possible (fall from height, head or neck injury, collision), keep the person still, support the head and neck in the position found, and call 997 or 911; do not move them unless in danger. For a suspected fracture, keep the limb still and support it. Minor sprains can be treated with rest, ice for 15 to 20 minutes, compression and elevation, with medical review if pain or swelling persists. `[TYPICAL]`
